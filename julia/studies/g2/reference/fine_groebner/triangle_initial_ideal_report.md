# Chevalley comparison for all 251 maximal cones

The original homogeneous ideal over `QQ` has dimension 6, degree 18, and is prime: **true**.

This is a fresh cone-by-cone computation grouped by the **32 label-induced Weyl orbits of `W(G2)`**, not by the obsolete 24 orbits of the larger abstract simplicial automorphism group. The Weyl group fixes taxon `7` and is generated by `(1 5 3 4 2 6)` and `(2 3)(5 6)`. Algebraic type and polytope data are constant on every one of these improved orbits.

The stored balanced weights use the min convention. In every cone table, `M2 weight` is the negative of the stored min-weight representative and can therefore be used with Macaulay2's `Weights => W`. The large Chevalley vector supplied by Bernd is itself written in min convention and is treated separately below.

For every prime toric cone, the script reconstructs the saturated intrinsic value lattice, computes the exact five-dimensional Newton--Okounkov polytope, and certifies equality of the cone relation lattice with the binomial initial-ideal relation lattice. `Chevalley = yes` means an exhaustive exact affine-unimodular equivalence test identifies the body with the lecture-hall simplex `P_(1,3,2,3,1)`; it is not an inference from the f-vector or volume.

Direct computations over all 251 relative-interior points give:

- 135 prime, binomial, monomial-free initial ideals (toric degenerations);
- 2 initial ideals prime over `QQ` but non-binomial;
- 114 non-prime initial ideals;
- 6 toric cones whose body is affinely unimodularly equivalent to the Chevalley simplex.

All initial ideals have dimension 6 and degree 18, and all 251 are monomial-free, hence all points lie in the tropical variety. The stronger test `T == T : (product of variables)` holds for 137 triangles; it fails for the other 114 because their initial ideals have an additional coordinate-boundary component. A Newton--Okounkov body from the Pluecker Khovanskii basis is recorded exactly for every toric case. The two prime non-binomial cases receive only the cone point polytope; it is deliberately not labelled a toric Newton--Okounkov body.
